### July 18th, 20XX
### Tokyo, Japan

**18 hours.**

Hikari, a brilliant student, has been studying for 18 hours.

*“It’s nothing,”* he thinks. 

*“I’ve been doing this forever now.”*

And he has.

For months, he’s been working *tirelessly*, pushing himself to the limit, focusing solely on his goal of entering a top university. Nothing else matters.

Nothing else **can** matter.

His friends and family have called, knocked; all shown concern, all worn the same expressions of worry. But Hikari has brushed them all aside, unconcerned with his own health. He’ll be fine. He’s ***always*** been fine.

Except…

`“...central Tokyo area continues to experience record-breaking heat. The temperature at noon today reached 40.8 degrees Celsius, setting a new all-time high temperature record for…”`

The radio drones on.

A heatwave. And on the day before the entrance exams, no less.

In his tiny, poorly ventilated apartment, Hikari feels like he’s in an oven. Each breath he takes feels like he’s inhaling fire. But he’s tuned it out. He’s tuned it all out, his focus set on something he’s deemed far more important than anything else.

**Studying.**

As the sun begins to set, he pauses. He grabs his head with one hand, feeling a splitting headache hit him hard. The heart palpitations and blurry vision were annoying enough, and now this?

He decides that now would be a good time to end this final cram session.

His skin, flushed to an unnatural and angry red, radiates with the overwhelming inner heat of his body. His chest burns, his rapid breathing only worsening the feeling of fire in his throat.  His eyes, bloodshot and dry, are no longer able to water.

He gets up. But just as he takes a step, he freezes. The burning in his chest suddenly crescendos into a sharp, stabbing pain. His vision blurs further as he falls over, a loud ringing audible in his ears. He reaches out and weakly grabs onto the edge of his desk, pulling some papers down along with him, heat stroke claiming him as he hits the ground with a loud *thud.*
